# Feedback lessons: what real rounds of notes taught

These rules come from making several motion videos with real requesters: an event promo (two script rounds and
three versions in one day), two vertical ads, and a product demo that took eight versions. Read them before phase 1.
They are defaults; the user's profile and notes win.

## How the rounds went (in short)

- **The request** was a "Dribbble-level UI motion" style (one shape morphing through UI states, cursor driven,
  springs, on the beat, looping), in the brand's style, with a voice-over, and a clear ask: estimate the cost first,
  and update the plan before making anything.
- **Script rounds** added concrete moments from the real programme, set the format (16:9, about 40 s), asked for a
  more fun tone, the CTA on the website, the price only on a sticker, and picked a voice from three test takes.
- **v1** opened with a "still doing it the old way?" question. Note: not convincing; start with the promise and be
  more enthusiastic. That sent it back to the script.
- **Script round 2** brought in the founders as real photo stickers (they sent a photo; no generated images) and
  fixed a join between two sentences that suggested something untrue.
- **v2** had one long voice take: it turned robotic halfway. The price showed in the opening: not wanted. The
  animations could be smoother and more over the top in the first seconds.
- **v3** fixed it with one take per sentence, checked with Whisper, an opening kit with bursts, slams and kicks, and
  the price only at the end.
- **The ads** added: no "made with AI" in ads (it devalued the offer), captions because people watch muted, vertical
  framing that is safe but not empty, characters drawn in code rather than generated people, tool names shown as
  their real logos, and no client names.
- **The product demo** added: caption style matters (outlined caps looked cheap on a light UI), the narrator's gender
  must match the voice, and a hook should show an outcome rather than describe the product.

## The rules this adds up to

1. The script comes first, as plain text, and the requester reads it before anything is voiced or built.
2. Open with the promise, with energy. No "still doing it the old way?" question hooks.
3. The first seconds are over the top: a cursor click that opens something, bursts, slams, kicks.
4. The price only at the end, on a sticker, never spoken.
5. Real people only as their own photos, with their OK. Types of people (a freelancer, a team lead) can be simple
   characters drawn in code (SVG), so arms, faces and props can move. No generated people.
6. Characters don't bounce on every beat: small hops on big moments only; let arms and faces do the work.
7. The sentences flow like people talk, and the joins don't suggest anything that isn't true.
8. One sentence per voice take, every take checked: robot artefacts and names are what go wrong.
9. The voice matches the narrator. A synthetic voice never pretends to be a real person.
10. Invite, don't lecture: describe what people discover and do; imperatives only in the CTA.
11. A targeted ad stays close to what the offer really includes. No client names, no promises the programme doesn't
    keep.
12. Build one hook, not two. Offer an A/B test, make it only when asked.
13. Ads get captions. Tools named in the copy show their real logos (if allowed).
14. Vertical: inside the safe zone, but not with the bottom third empty.
15. Look things up yourself; ask only about decisions, money and downloads.

## Add new rules

When a note turns out to be a general preference, write it into the user's `motion-video-profile.md` under
"Lessons", in their words plus the rule it adds up to.
