# Grill me: questions first, then the work

Goal: get the brief so sharp that the first version is right. Build or write **nothing** until the person has approved the summary in step 4.

## Step 1: look before you ask

Before asking anything, check what you can already know: the conversation, project instruction files (like `CLAUDE.md` or `AGENTS.md`), memory, files in the project, earlier versions, brand or style guides. Never ask something that's already there.

Open with one or two lines on what you already know and will assume, like "I'll use the tone and audience from your brand guide and the offer from the landing page you shared." The person can correct it in their answers.

## Step 2: one round of 10 to 15 questions

Ask all questions in one message, grouped by theme. Pick only what matters for this job:

- **Goal:** what should be different afterwards? How will you know it worked?
- **Audience:** who exactly, what do they already know, what are they unsure about?
- **Offer and core:** what is the one message that has to stick?
- **Proof:** which numbers, examples or experiences are real and can be used?
- **Format:** channel, format, length, language.
- **Limits:** what must stay out? Which words, claims or names are off limits?
- **Examples:** what do you like, and what do you definitely not want?
- **Practical:** deadline, who approves it, what already exists.

Rules for the questions:
- Number them, so someone can reply "3: yes, 7: no".
- Give **your own suggestion** in brackets with every question. Then "ok" is a valid answer too.
- Make them concrete. No open "tell me more". Prefer "Is the main message the early-bird price or the regular price? (suggestion: early bird, because the deadline is close)".
- Put the 3 questions that decide the most at the top.
- End the message by saying they can answer only the ones they care about, and you'll go with your suggestions for the rest.

Then stop and wait.

## Step 3: follow up where it stays vague

If an answer is vague or contradicts another one, ask at most 3 follow-up questions. No new round of 15.

## Step 4: play the brief back

Summarise everything in a short brief: goal, audience, core message, proof, format, limits and what you're going to make. Mark anything you filled in from your own suggestions, so the person sees what they didn't decide. Ask: "Is this right? Then I'll start." Only start after a yes.

If the project has a place for notes or briefs, offer to save the brief there so the next session can reuse it.

## What you don't do

- Don't ask questions whose answers are already in the conversation or files.
- Don't ask questions that wouldn't change the result.
- Don't quietly make a first version "to show the idea" before the brief is approved.
- For a small job (one sentence, a small fix), ask 3 questions at most. The point is a better brief, not getting through a questionnaire.
- Don't make up facts, numbers or examples in your suggestions. If you need proof you don't have, ask for it.
