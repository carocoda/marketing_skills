# Description guide

## Structure

1. **Link block.** First, every link the video mentions ("link's in the description"), in the order they come up. Label each one so it matches what the creator says on camera, for example `Free headline checker: https://...`. Then the standard lines from the channel profile (main offer, newsletter, a way to reach them). If the video mentions no links and the profile has no standard lines, start with the hook.
2. **Hook.** 2 or 3 lines. The outcome first, with the main search phrase in the first sentence.
3. **Context.** One short paragraph in the creator's tone on what the video walks through or what the catch is.
4. **What you'll learn.** 3 to 5 bullets. For videos under about 2 minutes, one tight paragraph is enough.
5. **Chapters.** Only for videos of about 10 minutes or more with 3 or more real moments. Format `0:00 Label`. Use real timestamps from the transcript, and tell the creator to check them against the final cut.
6. **Closing line.** The profile's sign-off or call to action, if it has one. Otherwise end with a plain invitation: "Questions? Leave a comment."
7. **Hashtags.** 2 or 3 exact-topic hashtags at the end.

Use `templates/youtube-package.md` for the layout.

## Why the link block goes first

Links at the top get clicked. Links under "...more" mostly don't. Because the link block leads, the label of the first link is the text people see above the fold on mobile. Make that label carry the search phrase when you can: `Free LinkedIn headline checker: https://...` puts "LinkedIn headline" above the fold.

If the creator would rather lead with the hook, follow their profile. Then the hook's first sentence carries the search phrase.

## Optional: a DM word to trace where people come from

Some creators end the link block with something like: `Connect with me on LinkedIn: [link] and send me "WORD" so I know you came from this video.` One unique word per video, in capitals, tied to the topic. When someone messages that word, the creator knows which video brought them.

Only use this if the creator wants it. If they keep a list of past words (a spreadsheet, Notion, Airtable), ask to see it or ask them to check that the new word isn't taken, and remind them to add the new one. If you have access to their tracking tool, offer to add it, and ask before writing.

## Search and length

- **The fold:** about 100 characters on mobile and 125 to 155 on desktop show before "...more". Line breaks count.
- **The description confirms, the video decides.** YouTube indexes what is said in the video, so the search phrase should be spoken early in the video. In the description, use it 2 or 3 times naturally, then variants. Keyword stuffing breaks YouTube's rules.
- **Length:** 150 to 300 words for a normal video, 100 to 200 for a short tutorial. Write each one fresh; never paste the same description under every video.
- **Short tutorials:** write the steps out as 2 to 4 numbered lines. A short video has a thin transcript, so the description does more of the work, and step text is what search snippets pick up.
- **Upload horizontal:** a video of 3 minutes or less in a square or vertical format becomes a Short, with a different feed and almost no search traffic. Tutorials meant for search should be 16:9.
- **Chapters:** at least 3, the first at `0:00`, each at least 10 seconds. Write chapter labels as short headlines (4 to 8 words) with variants of the search phrase. They show up in Google as "key moments".
- **Links:** links in the description don't hurt reach. Label every link so people know where it goes.
- **Hashtags:** 2 or 3 exact-topic tags at the end. YouTube shows up to 3 above the title, and it picks which. More than 60 and it ignores all of them. They're a minor feature, not a ranking lever.

## Tone

- Follow the tone from the channel profile.
- Lead with the outcome for the viewer, not a list of features.
- Only promise what the video shows. No results, numbers or names that aren't in it.
- Run the prose through the humanizer (or the quick check in SKILL.md). Links, bullets, chapters and hashtags are exempt.
