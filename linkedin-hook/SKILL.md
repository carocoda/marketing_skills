# LinkedIn hook: the two lines that decide everything

On mobile, LinkedIn shows roughly **140 characters** before "...see more". On desktop it's roughly 210. Everything that matters has to fit in there. For the reader, the rest of the post only exists if the hook works.

## Input

You need three things:

1. **The post**, or the idea for it.
2. **The audience:** who should stop scrolling for this?
3. **The goal:** be read, be saved, get comments, or get people to click your profile or a link.

If the post is there but the audience or goal is missing, ask for both in one short numbered message, with your guess in brackets so "ok" is a valid answer, like "2. Audience? (my guess: founders who do their own marketing)". No post at all? Ask for the topic and what the reader gets out of it. Then wait.

Skip any question whose answer is already in the conversation or project files.

## Proven mechanics

Pick one mechanic per hook. These come from outlier analyses of large AI and marketing creators, where posts with these openings did 2 to 3.5 times their author's normal results.

| Mechanic | How it works | Skeleton |
|---|---|---|
| Fear + fix | Something everyone recognises and would rather not have, and the post solves it | "Google your name. Now imagine a client doing that before your call." |
| Nobody ever told you | Promises the full picture the reader is missing | "Nobody ever explained [X] to you properly. Here it is." |
| Exactly what I use | A look inside someone's real setup | "These are the [N] [tools/prompts] I use every single day." |
| Something free up top | The value is already in the first line | "[N] free [X] that beat any paid course." |
| Number + result | Specific and measurable | "I analysed [N] [X]. [N] of them did this." |
| Contrarian | Goes against a common habit, with a reason | "Stop asking ChatGPT for its opinion." |
| You don't need X anymore | Replaces something people pay for | "You don't need [paid thing] if you use [tool] like this." |
| Recognisable moment | A concrete scene in the first sentence | "Yesterday I typed the same prompt for the fifth time." |

What helps: naming the tool or topic in the first line (like "Claude" or "Excel"), a concrete number, and something the reader can still do today. What flops: opinions without a usable example, news without an angle, vague promises.

## How to work

1. Read the post and decide what the reader really gets out of it. A hook may only promise what the post delivers.
2. Write 8 to 10 hooks using at least 5 different mechanics.
3. Count the characters of each hook up to the cut-off (count line breaks as characters, and use code to count if you can). Over 140? Shorten it, or break the second line so the curiosity sits right at the cut-off.
4. Give a top 3, with one sentence per hook on why it works for this audience and goal.
5. Make sure the second line connects to the rest of the post, for example "Here's the list:" or a line that makes the promise concrete.

## Rules

- Write in the language of the post, and make it sound native. A Dutch or German hook should not read like a translated English one.
- No em dashes, no emoji at the start, no capitals for shouting.
- No clickbait the post doesn't deliver. No made-up numbers: only numbers from the post or the person.
- No AI phrases ("game changer", "unlock", "in today's fast-paced world", "let's dive in").
- If the person wants, give the whole post a final read for phrases that sound machine-written once the hook is chosen.

## Output

```
1. [hook]  (mechanic, 118 characters)
2. ...

Top 3
- #4: why
- #1: why
- #7: why
```
