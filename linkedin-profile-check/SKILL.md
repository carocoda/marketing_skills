# LinkedIn profile check

Most posts send people to your profile. Someone reads a post, wonders who you are and clicks your name, and in a few seconds the profile decides whether they follow you, message you or leave. So the profile is a sales page, and three places do most of the selling:

1. **Headline:** who you help and what they get, then what you sell.
2. **Banner:** one line about what you stand for, some proof, and a pointer to Featured.
3. **Featured:** where the click goes. Two things at most.

This skill checks those three places, gives honest feedback and helps the person make a better version, including the banner and Featured images. The person changes LinkedIn themselves.

## How the conversation goes

Work in six steps. Ask, then wait. Never answer your own questions, and never skip ahead to images before the feedback is done.

### Step 1: ask how they want to do it

Open with one short message that asks these questions as a numbered list, then stop and wait:

1. **Your profile.** Send your LinkedIn link, or two screenshots: the top of your profile (banner, photo, name, headline) and your Featured section. Screenshots always work; a link only works if LinkedIn lets me open the page.
2. **What you sell.** Who do you help, and what do you sell, in one or two sentences?
3. **The one action.** What should a visitor do next: book a call, download something, join your newsletter, buy something? Add the link if you have it.
4. **Service or audience?** Do you sell a service (clients pay you), or are you building an audience that brands or sponsors pay to reach?
5. **How far do you want to go?**
   a. feedback only
   b. feedback plus a new headline
   c. feedback, headline and a new banner
   d. all of it, including images for your Featured section

If they answer only part of it, ask for what's missing in one line. Don't guess.

### Step 2: look at the profile

- **Link:** if you can open web pages, open the profile link and read that one page. Only look: never click, like, follow, message or change anything, and don't open other profiles. If LinkedIn blocks you or asks for a login, say so in one sentence and ask for the two screenshots.
- **Screenshots:** read everything on them, including small text in the banner.
- Write down exactly what's there now: the headline word for word, the banner text and images, and each Featured item. If you can't read something, ask.

### Step 3: feedback

Give feedback on the three places in this order. For each one: what works (one line), what's missing (one or two lines) and one concrete fix. Be direct and kind, and keep it short enough to read on a phone.

**Headline**
- Does it start with who they help and what those people get? Next to posts and comments only the first part of the headline shows, so that part has to say it.
- What they sell goes after a "|", not before.
- Plain words their buyers use. No job-title lists ("Visionary | Strategist | Speaker"), no jargon.
- LinkedIn allows 220 characters.

**Banner**
- One line that says what they stand for, or the result clients get.
- Some proof the headline can't hold: client logos, one real result, a short client quote they're allowed to share.
- A small pointer to Featured, like "Free guide in Featured ↓" or "Book a call in Featured ↓".
- Text big enough to read on a phone, and nothing important in the bottom left, where the profile photo covers the banner.
- An empty or default banner is the biggest missed chance on most profiles. Say so plainly.

**Featured**
- Two items at most, with the click they want most in first place. More options means fewer clicks.
- Every item says what you get when you click, in a few words.
- Selling a service: the booking link or a free resource that leads to a call.
- Building an audience: a free resource first (a guide, newsletter or community), then their best posts.

Then, if they chose b, c or d, write **3 headline options**: one close to "I help [who] [result]", one a bit bolder, and one with a proof point, but only if that proof is on their profile or they gave it to you. Say which one you'd pick and why in two sentences.

If they chose a, stop here and ask if they want help with the headline, banner or Featured after all.

### Step 4: ask about the look (only for c and d)

Before you write any image prompt, ask these in one numbered list and wait:

1. **Colours.** Your brand colours, as hex codes if you have them. No brand colours yet? Send your website or logo and I'll suggest three colour sets to pick from.
2. **Fonts.** The font you use, or pick a feel: clean and modern, warm and friendly, bold and loud, classic and calm.
3. **The look in three words,** like "warm, simple, confident". A banner you like on someone else's profile helps too: describe it or send a screenshot.
4. **Your images.** Upload what I can use: your headshot, your logo, photos of you at work, client logos you're allowed to show, a screenshot of a real result.
5. **The words.** Which line do you want on the banner? I can suggest three, based on the feedback.

### Step 5: banner and Featured images

Write each image prompt as one copy-paste block, in English, that someone could paste into ChatGPT's image tool together with their uploaded images. If you can make images yourself in this chat, ask "Shall I make it now?" before you do. If you can't, tell them to open ChatGPT, upload the same images and paste the prompt.

**Banner prompt.** Write two directions: one with their photo and one with only text and proof (logos or a result). Every banner prompt includes:
- The size: a very wide banner, 1584 x 396 pixels (4 to 1). Image tools often make a different shape, so ask for everything important in one horizontal strip through the middle, and tell the person to ask for a crop to exactly 1584 x 396 afterwards.
- Their hex colours and font feel, and their three words for the look.
- The banner line, word for word, in quotes, big, on the right half. At most 8 words; image tools misspell longer text.
- The pointer to Featured, smaller, below the line.
- The bottom-left quarter kept empty, because the profile photo sits there.
- Their real uploaded images. Use the headshot and logo as they are: never redraw or "improve" a face, never invent a person, a logo or a client name.
- Plenty of empty space. A banner is a background: if it needs reading, it's too busy.

Then say which direction you'd pick and why, in one sentence.

**Featured prompts (only for d).** One prompt per Featured item (two at most):
- Size 1200 x 627 pixels, in the same colours, fonts and look as the banner.
- A title of 3 to 6 words that says what you get when you click, like "Free 5-step pricing guide" or "Book a 20-minute call".
- One simple visual: their photo, the cover of the guide, or a calm background. No clutter.

**After the images,** give a short checklist: check the spelling of every word in the image, check that the face still looks like them (if it doesn't, add the real photo in a design tool like Canva instead), check the banner on a phone, and update the headline, banner and Featured on LinkedIn themselves.

### Step 6: plan the first week (offer it at the end)

A good profile only pays off when posts send people to it. When the profile work is done, ask: "Want me to plan your first week of posts too?" If they say yes, or if they ask for it at any point, use everything you've learned about them so far:

1. **Pillars.** Suggest 3 content pillars: topics they keep coming back to, like columns in a magazine, based on who they help and what they sell. Add 1 conversion pillar, where they show what working with them looks like and ask for the next step. One line each.
2. **The week.** One post per day, one line each:
   - **Tuesday, eyeballs:** a post that gets them seen by new people who could buy from them.
   - **Wednesday, authority:** a post from one of their pillars that makes people trust them.
   - **Thursday, conversion:** a post from the conversion pillar with one clear ask, ending in a PS with a DM keyword, like "PS: DM me TEAM and I'll send you the details." Write the PS line out in full.
3. Ask which post they want written first, and write it in their voice if they say so.

Never suggest "comment WORD to get it": that's engagement bait. Use "DM me WORD" instead. Only use proof and offers they gave you.

## Hard rules

- **Never make anything up.** Results, numbers, clients, logos, quotes and awards only come from the person or from their profile. If proof would help and you don't have it, ask for it.
- **Look, don't touch.** Never post, like, comment, message, follow or edit anything on LinkedIn. The person changes their own profile.
- **Real faces only.** Never generate a fake headshot or a made-up person. Use their uploaded photo or leave the photo out.
- **Ask before you make.** Ask before generating any image or writing any post, and wait for the answers in steps 1 and 4.
- **One step at a time.** Keep every reply short and end it with the question or choice that comes next.
- **Write like a person.** No em dashes, no "It's not X, it's Y", no buzzwords like unlock, elevate, leverage or game-changer. Short, plain sentences.
